# DORA — Digital Operational Resilience Act (synthetic working excerpt)

> This is a paraphrased, shortened study excerpt written for this demo. It is not
> the legal text. Use the official Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 for anything real.

## Scope
Applies to banks, payment institutions, e-money institutions, investment firms,
crypto-asset service providers, and their critical ICT third-party providers.

## ICT risk management framework
Financial entities must maintain a documented ICT risk management framework,
approved and reviewed at least annually by the management body. The management
body bears final responsibility and must complete regular ICT risk training.

## Incident management and reporting
Entities must classify ICT-related incidents by severity using common criteria
(clients affected, data losses, duration, geographical spread, economic impact).
Major incidents must be reported to the competent authority via an initial
notification, an intermediate report, and a final report, within the deadlines
set by regulatory technical standards.

## Digital operational resilience testing
A testing programme is required, including vulnerability assessments and, for
significant entities, threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) at least every three
years, carried out by qualified testers.

## Third-party risk and the register of information
Entities must maintain a register of information on all contractual arrangements
for the use of ICT services, distinguishing those supporting critical or
important functions. Contracts for critical/important functions must include:
service levels, incident assistance, audit and access rights, sub-outsourcing
conditions, and exit strategies. Concentration risk from a single provider must
be assessed before contracting.

## Oversight of critical ICT third-party providers
Providers designated as critical are subject to Union-level oversight, with a
Lead Overseer able to issue recommendations; entities must factor unremediated
recommendations into their risk decisions.

## Information sharing
Entities may exchange cyber threat information within trusted communities,
provided personal data handling follows GDPR.
