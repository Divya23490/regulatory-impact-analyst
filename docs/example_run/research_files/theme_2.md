# Theme 2: ICT-Related Incident Reporting

## Regulatory Requirements
- Financial entities must establish and implement an ICT-related incident management process to detect, manage, and notify incidents, incorporating early warning indicators and incident logging/classification procedures [dora::art17::p3].
- Financial entities are required to report major ICT-related incidents to the relevant competent authority using prescribed templates and timelines [dora::art19::p1.1].
- The reporting lifecycle comprises: (a) an initial notification, (b) intermediate reports upon significant status changes, and (c) a final report upon completion of root-cause analysis and actual impact assessment [dora::art19::p4].
- Financial entities must inform affected clients without undue delay when a major incident impacts their financial interests, and may voluntarily notify significant cyber threats [dora::art19::p2, dora::art19::p3].

## Affected Internal Policies
- [policy::POL-003] — Incident Management Standard: Defines internal incident severity scales (P1-P4) but lacks regulatory notification workflows, timelines, and standardized reporting templates.
- [policy::POL-004] — Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery: Covers RTO/RPO and annual DR testing, but lacks incident communication and client disclosure procedures required under DORA Art. 17(3)(d) and 19(3).

## Gap Analysis
- **Regulator Notification Workflow**: POL-003 lacks the mandatory multi-stage incident reporting pipeline (initial, intermediate, final reports) and strict regulatory timelines.
- **Client Notification**: Current procedures do not mandate notifying clients without undue delay when major incidents affect their financial interests [dora::art19::p3].
- **Threat Notification**: No internal workflow exists for voluntary reporting of significant cyber threats to competent authorities [dora::art19::p2].
